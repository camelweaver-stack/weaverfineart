**A Fruit Piece** is a mezzotint by Richard Earlom after the 1723 painting by Jan van Huysum, published in London by John Boydell in 1781. It is one of the Houghton Gallery prints, Boydell's series reproducing the pictures that Sir Robert Walpole had collected at Houghton Hall in Norfolk. Grapes, peaches, nuts and other fruit lie on a marble ledge before a bouquet in a vase with classical reliefs, with the Walpole coat of arms below.

This is a **lettered impression**: it carries its title, the Walpole arms with their motto, and the credits to the painter, the draughtsman Joseph Farington, the engraver and the publisher. It is not an early proof before letters. Those early states are scarcer, and the British Museum and Donald Heald Gallery describe them as printed before the title and before the motto was added to the arms. The washes of color appear to be later hand-coloring, not part of the original printing.

**The paintings and the sale.** Walpole, Britain's first prime minister, bought van Huysum's work in the painter's lifetime. In 1779 his grandson, the 3rd Earl of Orford, sold the greater part of the Houghton pictures to Catherine the Great of Russia to pay debts. The two van Huysum paintings, a flower piece dated 1722 and this flower and fruit piece dated 1723, went with them and are now in the State Hermitage Museum in St Petersburg (the 1723 painting is inventory number ГЭ-1049, 80 × 60.5 cm). A valuation made for the sale by the painter Benjamin West is reported to have put the pair of van Huysums at £1,200.

**The series.** Boydell began the Houghton project in the early 1770s, and the two-volume set, dedicated to Catherine II, reproduces 129 of the pictures. Its plates carry publication dates from 1774 to 1788, and this one is among the plates dated 1781. The British Museum notes that the flower and fruit pair quickly became celebrated, and that Earlom re-engraved the plates to meet demand, the second version being judged not as good as the first.
