Two barefoot children stand in a meadow of tall grass and yellow wildflowers. The older girl, in a pale dress with a white ribbon in her ponytail, holds the skirt up to carry the blooms she has picked. The younger boy, in a white shirt and brown shorts, reaches toward more stems with one hand and holds a few behind his back. Both have lowered eyes and softly painted faces. The picture is built from loose vertical washes of chartreuse, golden yellow and olive green, with darker, scraped grass strokes in the lower third and small flicks of yellow for the flowers.

It is signed "J. Greenleaf" and is attributed to Jean Greenleaf of Campbell, California, a painter of the 1960s and 1970s. A second oil by the same hand, a girl in a meadow overlooking sailboats, came from the same Goodwill in San Jose. Very little is recorded about the artist, and the artist page sets out exactly what the gallery has and has not been able to confirm. The painting hangs in a heavy carved gilt frame, 36 by 30 inches, with a linen liner.
