Jean Greenleaf is known to the gallery from signed oils and from a short trail in local newspapers. Her full name rests on a pencil inscription on the stretcher bar of a landscape titled *Blue Lake*, signed "J. Greenleaf" and dated 1962, which reads "Jean Greenleaf" with an address and telephone number in Campbell, California. That 28 by 22 inch oil was offered in 2026 by a San Jose dealer.

The same address and telephone number lead to a painter whom Santa Clara Valley newspapers name Regina Greenleaf. The Los Gatos Times profiled her in 1963 during American Art Week: she had come to the valley when her husband took a position at San Jose State College, and in 1960 she took up oil painting in an adult education class. She was a member of the Los Gatos Art Association from 1961, serving as an officer and publicity chair and judging its shows, and she took third place in oils at the West Valley Historical Society's 1968 exhibit in Campbell. A 1975 notice calls her first president and an organizer of the Campbell Artists Guild, edited its newsletter, and says she taught oil painting at an adult education center in San Jose, demonstrated the encaustic (wax) method, and favored "people and flowers." In 1979 she became president of the Saratoga Contemporary Artists. Those notices are the basis for the dates given above.

The gallery has not confirmed that the "Jean" of the stretcher and the "Regina" of the newspapers are the same person, or why two first names appear; the match rests on the shared address and telephone number and on the timing, since she was painting in oils by 1962. It has also not seen a work that names her as Regina. *Children in a Flower Field* carries the signature "J. Greenleaf" and shows children among flowers, a subject that fits her stated preference, but a subject is not a record. The gallery has found no birth or death dates and no auction record for her. Other painters named Greenleaf, including a Saratoga artist named Janet Greenleaf active in the same circles, appear to be different people. This page will be updated if more is found.
