Richard Earlom was born in London and baptized on 14 May 1743. He studied drawing under the painter Giovanni Battista Cipriani and taught himself mezzotint, the engraving method in which a copper plate is roughened all over and then burnished to bring up the lights. In 1765 the print publisher John Boydell employed him to draw the pictures at Houghton Hall in Norfolk, the great collection of Sir Robert Walpole, so that they could be engraved.

For Boydell he made two of the best-known print projects of the 1770s. The first was the *Liber Veritatis*, a collected edition of prints after all 200 drawings in Claude Lorrain's own record book of his paintings, published between 1774 and 1777. Earlom used etching for Claude's pen lines and mezzotint for the washes. The British Museum's curator notes that these prints, more than the drawings themselves, spread Claude's influence among British artists. The second was Boydell's *Houghton Gallery*, a series of prints after the Walpole pictures, which the Art Institute of Chicago credits with 27 plates by Earlom.

His flower and fruit pieces after the Dutch painter **Jan van Huysum** (1682–1749) are usually counted among his finest plates. The two paintings, one dated 1722 and one 1723, hung at Houghton until 1779, when Walpole's grandson sold the collection to Catherine the Great. They are now in the State Hermitage Museum in St Petersburg. Earlom's prints of them were published in 1778 and 1781, and the British Museum notes that they were in such demand that he re-engraved the plates. Earlom also engraved subjects after Johan Zoffany, Joseph Wright of Derby and Sir Joshua Reynolds. He died in London on 9 October 1822.
