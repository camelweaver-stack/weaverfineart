**A Fruit Piece** is a mezzotint by Richard Earlom after Jan van Huysum's painting of 1723, published by John Boydell for the Houghton Gallery. The lettered impression at the [MAK, Vienna](https://sammlung.mak.at/en/collect/fruechtestilleben-herausgegeben-von-john-boydell_221671), is dated 1 September 1781 and carries Boydell's Cheapside address. Some catalogues give 1 November 1781 instead. The line on this sheet has not yet been read out of the frame. This impression is a fully lettered published state, with the title, the credits, Boydell's Cheapside address, and the Walpole arms with the motto *Fari quae sentiat*. The color is later hand-coloring. It is not a proof before letters, a date of 1781 on the plate does not show that this sheet was pulled in that year, and the coloring is not contemporary with the printing.

**The paintings.** Jan van Huysum painted the Flower Piece in 1722 and this Fruit Piece in 1723. Both hung in Sir Robert Walpole's collection at Houghton Hall, Norfolk, among the finest picture collections in Britain.

**The 1779 sale.** Walpole's grandson, George, 3rd Earl of Orford, sold the greater part of the Houghton collection in 1779 to Catherine II of Russia. The two van Huysum paintings went to St Petersburg. They remain in the State Hermitage Museum. Boydell had already begun a print project to record the collection. Sir Robert Walpole and, after 1779, Catherine the Great are the notable historical owners of the paintings. John Boydell is the publisher of the prints. None of the three is a recorded owner of this sheet.

**The drawings and the plates.** Joseph Farington, with others, made the drawings from the paintings, and Earlom scraped the mezzotints. Boydell issued the plates in batches of ten between 1774 and 1788, then published the collected work in two elephant-folio volumes on 1 January 1788, with Josiah Boydell, under the title *A Set of Prints Engraved after the Most Capital Paintings in the Collection of Her Imperial Majesty, the Empress of Russia, Lately in the Possession of the Earl of Orford at Houghton in Norfolk*, dedicated to Catherine II. Most plates keep their earlier dates. The 1788 title pages do not mean this Fruit Piece was first printed in 1788. The Flower Piece plate is dated 25 June 1778. The [Royal Academy's set](https://www.royalacademy.org.uk/art-artists/book/a-set-of-prints-engraved-after-the-most-capital-paintings-in-the-collection) describes 129 pictures, with the van Huysums as volume I, plates 59 and 60, plus plans and elevations. The [Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/109155/a-fruit-piece-from-the-houghton-gallery) describes the finished book as 162 prints, including 27 by Earlom. An [Eton College copy](https://catalogue.etoncollege.com/B32188) has 162 plates on 133 leaves. The two counts agree if one is pictures only and the other includes frontispieces, plans and titles.

**Demand and re-engraving.** Boydell's table of contents, quoted on the Smithsonian's impressions from George P. Marsh's copy of volume I, said these two plates surpassed, in point of execution, every print before engraved in this manner. They were among the most admired plates in the set and were also sold as a pair of decorative prints. Demand was high enough that the plates were re-engraved. A [British Museum curator's note](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1838-0420-206) on its Fruit Piece, citing Duchesne's 1823 account of prints in the Royal Library in Paris, says the second version was judged inferior to the first. Wessely numbered the first plates 144 (Flower) and 145 (Fruit) and the later plates 146 and 147. The motto in the arms is not enough to tell the two families of plates apart. [Harvard holds a Fruit Piece catalogued as Wessely 147](https://harvardartmuseums.org/collections/object/275220).

**Why early impressions are prized.** A mezzotint's velvety burr, which makes an early proof rich, wears down with printing, so later pulls look flatter. Proofs before the title, and impressions taken before the motto was added to the arms, are scarcer than fully lettered impressions, and they turn up less often. Yale holds a [Fruit Piece progress proof](https://collections.britishart.yale.edu/catalog/tms:22237) made before the mezzotint was finished. This sheet is the published, fully lettered state. Lettered impressions of these plates are not unique. A 1778 or 1781 date on the plate stayed there when impressions were printed later for the 1788 volumes, so the date does not by itself prove a first-year pull. Whether this is a late pull from the first plate or an impression from the re-engraved plate cannot be decided until the sheet is compared out of the frame. It is a copperplate mezzotint, not one of the later photogravure reproductions of the image.

**Impressions in public collections.** There is no published census. The list below is a lower bound of impressions confirmed from official records, plus bound sets that still contain the plates. Institutions often hold one of the pair, both, or a proof as well as a lettered state.

- [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1838-0420-206), Fruit Piece, published state. The Museum also holds a [Flower Piece](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1842-0806-207) that is a proof before the title.
- [Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/109155/a-fruit-piece-from-the-houghton-gallery), Fruit Piece, and the [Flower Piece](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/106764/a-flower-piece-from-the-houghton-gallery).
- [Yale Center for British Art](https://collections.britishart.yale.edu/catalog/tms:38874), Fruit Piece, Paul Mellon Collection, and a [progress proof](https://collections.britishart.yale.edu/catalog/tms:22237) of the Fruit Piece. Yale also holds the [Flower Piece](https://collections.britishart.yale.edu/catalog/tms:38869).
- [Harvard Art Museums](https://harvardartmuseums.org/collections/object/311929), a lettered Fruit Piece, the later-plate [Fruit Piece, Wessely 147](https://harvardartmuseums.org/collections/object/275220), and a [Flower Piece](https://harvardartmuseums.org/collections/object/312030).
- [Smithsonian, National Museum of American History](https://www.si.edu/object/fruit-piece%3Anmah_780455), Fruit Piece from George P. Marsh's volume, removed from the binding for framing in 1894. The companion [Flower Piece](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_784894) was removed from that volume in 1888.
- [Rijksmuseum](https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200148924), Fruit Piece, van den Velden Bequest, 1892, and the [Flower Piece](https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200148925).

- [Minneapolis Institute of Art (Mia)](https://collections.artsmia.org/art/48540/a-fruit-piece-richard-earlom), Fruit Piece.
- [MAK, Vienna](https://sammlung.mak.at/en/collect/fruechtestilleben-herausgegeben-von-john-boydell_221671), lettered Fruit Piece dated 1 September 1781, with Boydell's Cheapside address.
- [Staatliche Kunsthalle Karlsruhe](https://www.kunsthalle-karlsruhe.de/kunstwerke/Richard-Earlom/Fr%C3%BCchtestillleben/CAFE8F0E470FF97782BA7894187B1FA0/), Fruit Piece, 1781.
- [Musée du Louvre, Edmond de Rothschild Collection](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl020543848), Fruit Piece, given in 1935, and the [Flower Piece](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl020543849).
- [Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/prints/item/264917), Flower Piece. State and measurements were not available from that page.

Intact 1788 volumes at the [Royal Academy](https://www.royalacademy.org.uk/art-artists/book/a-set-of-prints-engraved-after-the-most-capital-paintings-in-the-collection) and at [Eton](https://catalogue.etoncollege.com/B32188) still have these plates in the binding. Other sets were broken up. The Smithsonian's Marsh impressions were taken out of the volume so they could be exhibited and framed. Once extracted, sheets of this kind were often hand-colored by a later hand and put in gilt frames.
