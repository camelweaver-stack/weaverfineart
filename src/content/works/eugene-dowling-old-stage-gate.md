In October 1953 Eugene Dowling of Portland, Oregon painted a weathered plank gate set in a sunlit adobe wall, and titled it "Old Stage Gate, Ramona's Marriage Place." For more than half a century, Ramona's Marriage Place was the name every tourist knew for the Casa de Estudillo in Old Town San Diego. The adobe was begun in 1827 for the presidio commander José María de Estudillo. After Helen Hunt Jackson's novel *Ramona* (1884) made the house famous, it was restored and opened to visitors in 1910 as the place where the novel's heroine was said to have married. A gate in the far corner of its walled courtyard let carriages and horses in, and a photograph from about 1890 shows a stagecoach standing in the patio.

The painting is attributed to Eugene H. Dowling (1879–1961), a Portland attorney whose name appears in pencil on the back. His wife, Colista Murray Dowling, was one of Portland's best-known painters and an officer of the Oregon Society of Artists. The painting came to us from Oregon, and Dowling's typed entry tag from a 1953 show, priced at $50, is still on the back.
